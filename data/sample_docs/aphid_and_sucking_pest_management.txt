# Chapter 1: Identification, Biology & Crop Damage

Aphids (Aphis gossypii Glover, Myzus persicae Sulzer) belong to the family Aphididae (Order: Hemiptera). They are tiny, soft-bodied, pear-shaped sucking insects (1.5–2.5 mm in length) ranging from yellowish-green to dark olive green or black.

### Mode of Damage:
1. Direct Sucking of Plant Sap: Both nymphs and adults cluster densely in colonies on the ventral surface of young tender leaves, terminal shoots, and floral buds.
2. Distortion & Leaf Cupping: Heavy feeding causes downward curling, crinkling, chlorotic mottling, and severe stunting of vegetative growth.
3. Sooty Mold Development: Aphids excrete copius quantities of sweet, glistening honeydew. This honeydew serves as a substrate for the black sooty mold fungus (Capnodium spp.), which covers leaf laminae and drastically impairs photosynthetic efficiency.
4. Viral Disease Vectoring: Aphids serve as highly efficient vectors transmitting devastating plant viruses, including Cucumber Mosaic Virus (CMV), Potato Virus Y (PVY), and Papaya Ringspot Virus.

# Chapter 2: Economic Threshold Levels (ETL) & Scouting Protocol

### Field Scouting Procedure:
- Inspect 20 randomly selected plants across a zigzag or 'W' transect twice weekly during morning hours.
- Examine 3 leaves per plant (top, middle, and bottom canopy).

### Economic Threshold Levels (ETL):
- Cotton: 10% affected plants or 15–20 aphids per leaf.
- Vegetables (Tomato / Chilli / Brinjal): 5–10 aphids per leaf before flowering stage.
- Brassica / Mustard: 20–25 aphids per 10 cm terminal central shoot or 1.5 cm aphid colony length.

# Chapter 3: Integrated Pest Management (IPM) & Recommended Interventions

### Cultural & Mechanical Controls:
- Install Yellow Sticky Traps @ 15–20 traps/acre at crop canopy level to monitor and mass-trap winged alates.
- Conserve natural predators including Ladybird beetles (Coccinella septempunctata, Cheilomenes sexmaculata), Syrphid hoverfly larvae, and Chrysoperla carnea.
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization, which produces succulent lush foliage highly attractive to sucking pests.

### Biological Control:
- Foliar spray of entomopathogenic fungus *Verticillium lecanii* (1x10^8 CFU/g) or *Beauveria bassiana* @ 5 g/liter of water in late afternoon under high relative humidity (>80%).

### Certified Chemical Control (Package of Practices):
- **Imidacloprid 17.8% SL**: 0.3 ml per liter of water (50–60 ml/acre).
- **Acetamiprid 20% SP**: 0.2 g per liter of water (40 g/acre).
- **Flonicamid 50% WG**: 0.3 g per liter of water (60 g/acre) — selective against sucking pests and safe for pollinators/natural enemies.
- **Thiamethoxam 25% WG**: 0.25 g per liter of water (40–50 g/acre).
- Always ensure complete spray coverage directed at the undersides of the leaves.
